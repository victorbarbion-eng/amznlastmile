## 2-minute version (for the classroom / jury Q&A)

**Hook.** Amazon wants Amazon Shipping to become the AWS of logistics. In Spain — a €4-billion last-mile market — that means winning big enterprise deals. But every one of those deals starts as a mess: a formal RFQ here, a folder of contradictory CRM notes and emails there. Today a Business Developer spends 30 to 40% of their time just reconciling that information before they can even recommend a price.

**Problem.** It's slow, it's inconsistent, and the biggest deals are lost for a reason the data makes obvious: 58% of past losses were service or geographic gaps — the customer wanted something Amazon Shipping simply can't deliver, and nobody sized it early enough.

**Solution.** ShipSense is an Enterprise Opportunity Copilot. Paste in an RFQ or a discovery pack and it produces nine things a BD needs: an executive summary, an opportunity score, a risk assessment, three pricing scenarios that respect our margin guardrails, a commercial strategy, the exact follow-up questions to ask the client, a win-probability benchmarked against 360 historical deals, a client-ready proposal, and a full sources trail.

**Proof it's grounded.** For Tecnomania it strips out the 16% of volume we can't serve and sizes the real deal at 2.45 million parcels a year. For Pink Papaya it reads five contradictory emails, reconciles them, and flags that France — 18% of their volume and a co-founder's hard requirement — is outside our coverage and our number-one risk. The win-probability model isn't a guess: internationally-dependent deals historically win 35% of the time versus 62% otherwise.

**Why it's the right AI.** The scoring and pricing are rule-based — transparent and auditable, which is exactly what a compliance-sensitive B2B pricing decision needs. The LLM does what it's best at: reading messy documents and writing the summary, the risks and the proposal. A human always signs off.

**Impact.** Days of analysis become minutes. Decisions become consistent. And a BD walks into every meeting already knowing the real deal size, the right price, and the honest answer to the customer's hardest question.
